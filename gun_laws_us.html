Common travel questions answered

  • Reciprocity? Varies wildly. Your home permit is often not honored in Red/Yellow states. Always check before crossing borders.
  • Vehicle carry? Many states require guns unloaded & cased for non-residents. Some allow loaded in glove box.
  • Rifles? Open carry of long guns is legal without permit in 45+ states. Concealed long guns are rare.
  • Prohibited places? Schools, courthouses, airports, bars (varies), private property “no guns” signs (enforced in some states).
  • Retired LEOs (e.g. Utah retirees): Federal LEOSA grants concealed carry rights in ALL 50 states + territories. Carry your agency photo ID + recent qualification cert.

Most Common Things Travelers Should Know

1. Reciprocity is NOT universal

Even if your home state issues a permit, only certain states honor it. Red and many Yellow states often do not recognize out-of-state permits. Permitless states usually don’t issue permits at all.

2. You must follow the laws of the state you’re in

Your home permit or permitless status does not override stricter local rules. “I didn’t know” is not a defense.

3. Vehicle transport rules differ

Some states require handguns unloaded and in a locked case in the trunk. Others allow loaded in the glovebox. Rifles usually easier.

4. Prohibited locations are common

Federal gun-free school zones + state bans on courthouses, airports, stadiums, bars (BAC limits), and any posted private property.

5. Duty to inform & stand-your-ground

Some states require you to tell an officer you’re armed during a traffic stop. Stand-your-ground / castle doctrine laws vary widely.

6. Age & eligibility

Most states require you to be 21+ (some 18+ in Green states). Federal law prohibits certain people from possessing firearms.

Special Note for Retired Law Officers (e.g., Utah retirees with Utah credentials)

Under the federal Law Enforcement Officers Safety Act (LEOSA / HR 218), qualified retired law enforcement officers have the right to carry a concealed handgun in all 50 states and U.S. territories — regardless of state laws or permit status. You must carry your agency-issued photographic ID and proof of annual firearms qualification with the same type of firearm you are carrying.
